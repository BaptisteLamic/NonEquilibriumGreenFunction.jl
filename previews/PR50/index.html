NonEquilibriumGreenFunction

Solving the non-equilibrium Dyson equation in the time domain with quasi-linear complexity.

This package is the research code accompanying:

  1. The thesis: Quantum transport in voltage-biased Josephson junctions
  2. The paper: Solving the Transient Dyson Equation with Quasilinear Complexity via Matrix Compression

Overview

NonEquilibriumGreenFunction.jl solves the transient Dyson equation

\[G = g + g \Sigma G\]

on a discretized time axis. Time-domain discretization turns the integral equation into a linear system whose naive cost scales as $\mathcal O(N^3)$ in the number of time steps. By compressing the kernel matrices (HSS compression, or FFT-accelerated circulant structure for stationary kernels), the cost drops to $\mathcal O(N \log N)$.

Typical use case

A simulation always follows the same pipeline, regardless of the physics:

  1. Discretize the kernels: the bare retarded Green function g, the lead self-energies Σ (built from LocalKernel for local (contact) terms and RetardedKernel / AcausalKernel for continuous ones), and the thermal occupation ρ (an acausal kernel).
  2. Solve the retarded Dyson equation: solve_dyson(g, g * Σ_R) returns the full retarded Green function G_R.
  3. Dress the kinetic branch: build the Keldysh self-energy Σ_K from the lead couplings and ρ, then G_K = G_R * Σ_K * G_R'.
  4. Extract observables: combine G_R, G_K and the lead self-energies into currents or densities.

The two worked examples cover the two common flavours (see the Examples section of the manual):

  • Metal - Quantum Dot - Metal junction: scalar (bs=1) kernels in equilibrium and under a voltage bias, including a complexity benchmark.
  • Superconductor - Quantum Dot - Superconductor junction: Nambu (bs=2) kernels with superconducting leads, Stationary circulant compression, and the transient current response to a voltage ramp.
